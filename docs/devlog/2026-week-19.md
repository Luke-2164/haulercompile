# Weekly Dev Log - 2026 Week 19

## Project

**haulercompile**

haulercompile is a renewal tracking app for small trucking businesses. The goal is to help operators prevent missed renewals for insurance, permits, truck documents, and driver documents.

## Overview

This week, I worked on improving the MVP workflow and clarifying the product direction.

The main progress was not just adding screens. I also reviewed whether the product should compete as a general task management app or focus more narrowly on a painful business problem: missed renewals.

## What I worked on

### 1. Master data list pages

I completed and verified basic list pages for the core master data used by the renewal workflow.

- Companies
- Trucks
- Drivers

These pages make it easier to confirm which records have already been created before linking them to renewals.

This improves the basic workflow:

```text
Create company
Create truck or driver
Create renewal
Review linked records
```

### 2. Archive workflow review

I reviewed the current Archive behavior for renewals.

The current approach uses a soft-delete style workflow instead of permanently deleting data.

Conceptually, archived renewals are hidden from active views while remaining in the database.

This is useful for avoiding accidental permanent deletion and keeps the door open for future restore or audit features.

### 3. Product positioning review

I reconsidered the product's positioning.

A generic task management app would be difficult to differentiate because many free or low-cost alternatives already exist, such as spreadsheets, calendars, reminders, and task apps.

The stronger direction is to position haulercompile as:

> A missed-renewal prevention tool for small trucking businesses.

The value should not be generic task tracking. The value should be reducing the risk of missed renewals, fines, downtime, and operational confusion.

### 4. Renew workflow discovery

One important product insight this week was that Archive may not be the most important action for an expired renewal.

When a document expires, the real user action may be:

> The document has been renewed, so I need to set the next expiration date.

Because of this, I identified **Renew / Update next expiration date** as a likely next core feature.

A simple first version could update the existing renewal with a new expiration date. A more advanced version may eventually store renewal history, but that can wait until the workflow is validated.

### 5. Demand validation direction

I also refined the validation strategy.

Since speaking and listening in English are still areas I am improving, I decided not to rely on calls at the beginning.

Instead, the first validation approach will be text-based:

- Email
- Website contact forms
- LinkedIn messages
- Short survey forms
- Relevant online communities

The first outreach should focus on learning, not selling.

Example direction:

```text
I’m researching how small trucking businesses manage renewals for insurance, permits, trucks, and driver documents. Could I ask 3 quick questions by email?
```

Adding a line like this should reduce friction:

```text
No call needed — email replies are totally fine.
```

## Technical progress

- Confirmed master data list pages for companies, trucks, and drivers.
- Reviewed renewal archive behavior as a soft-delete workflow.
- Clarified that active renewal views should only show active records.
- Identified that a future Renew workflow may need updates to expiration dates and possibly renewal history.

## Product decisions

- Do not compete as a generic task management app.
- Focus on missed-renewal prevention for small trucking businesses.
- Treat Archive as a supporting workflow.
- Treat Renew / Update next expiration date as a higher-value workflow to investigate.
- Start demand validation through text-based outreach instead of calls.

## What I learned

### Technical

Soft delete is a practical MVP approach for Archive because it avoids permanent data loss and keeps future restore options open.

However, for the main business workflow, updating the next expiration date may be more valuable than hiding the record.

### Product

The strongest value proposition is not convenience. It is loss prevention.

A small trucking business may not pay for another task app, but it may care about preventing missed renewals that can cause fines, downtime, or administrative stress.

### Go-to-market

Early validation does not require sales calls.

Text-based outreach can still test whether the pain exists, especially if the request is small and specific.
